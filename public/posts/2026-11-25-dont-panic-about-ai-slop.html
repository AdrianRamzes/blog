Don't Panic About AI Slop

Disclaimer: the views in this post are my own and do not represent those of my employer.

Today’s friction has sparked a wave of outrage, hot takes, and manifestos like the No AI Slop Manifesto.

Don’t panic. Today’s models are the worst we will ever use - as better ones arrive, all of this drama will age like milk.

We Are Engineers, Not Artists

Every experienced developer has seen horrific code, and we have also seen code so clean, clever, and expressive that it feels like fine art. But we are not getting paid for art. We are engineers: we build stuff, solve problems, and build stuff to solve problems. For our users - for the people actually paying - the elegance of a class hierarchy does not matter at all.

“I do not love the bright sword for its sharpness, nor the arrow for its swiftness, nor the warrior for his glory. I love only that which they defend.”

- Faramir, The Two Towers by J.R.R. Tolkien

Compare the world without our solution to the world with it. Saving people time or money creates a gap between the two - and our reward is simply a slice of that difference.

Why Code Slop Is Different

In a few years, “slop” - or some new, different word - will still describe low-effort AI media nobody asked for: an awful song, image, book, game, or video that took less time to generate than it takes a human to sit through.

Software is fundamentally different. With media, the generated artifact is the product. With code, the artifact stays hidden under the hood. As long as it solves a real problem reliably, ugliness inside the file hurts nobody.

The Real Risk and the Spaghetti Monster

Today, AI writes code - sometimes well, often as messy “slop” that needs heavy guidance. Ugliness isn’t the danger. When we don’t understand the code inside our product, its side effects become much less predictable - corrupting data, leaking secrets, or breaking an unrelated service.

Whether the speed we gain justifies that risk is an open question on every PR we merge. Still, as models and sandboxes mature, those side effects will become far more predictable and contained.

Besides, every developer already knows the classic warning at the top of a cursed legacy file:

//
// Dear maintainer:
//
// Once you are done trying to 'optimize' this routine,
// and have realized what a terrible mistake that was,
// please increment the following counter as a warning
// to the next guy:
//
// total_hours_wasted_here = 42
//

AI will produce far more spaghetti than humans ever did. The difference is that it brings its own Spaghetti Monster. The same AI that writes tangled, unreadable code will debug, rewrite, and maintain that tangle on its own - without any human needing to understand how it works inside.

The Leap

Think back to C++ and Assembly. Early C++ compilers produced bloated, obfuscated Assembly - far longer than what a skilled Assembly programmer wrote by hand. Today, nobody cares, because nobody looks at the Assembly anymore.

Of course, historical abstractions weren’t identical to AI. Every earlier rung up the abstraction ladder - from machine code on a CPU to Assembly, C++, Java, and Python - translated strict logic into strict logic, compacting deterministic rules into a leaner form easier for programmers to read.

Then comes the leap where you meet AI - and you don’t need strict logical code anymore. You state a goal in messy human language, AI invents the algorithm and writes formal code, and down at the bottom CPUs still execute deterministic instructions. That leap is wild.

The Last Code Review

Why did we invent high-level languages, classes, and object-oriented programming in the first place? Because our limited human brains are not comfortable with machine code. Abstractions were never for the machine - they were for us.

AI has no such limitation. There is no reason why it shouldn’t write Assembly directly - or whatever form is cheapest to run and verify. When that happens, all those textbooks on software engineering become a pile of outdated paper.

Once models mature and earn our trust, code written entirely by AI won’t be reviewed by anyone - reading machine-maintained code won’t create value for a single person. Instead, we will focus entirely on effects and risk analysis: defining the right problems, verifying results, and keeping side effects in bounds without ever opening the code.

Rolling the Tech Debt Forward

Right now, we are stuck in the awkward middle: models generate mountains of code, yet humans still have to read messy diffs, contain side effects, and guide them by hand.

Yes, we are taking on the biggest tech debt in the history of software. But this time we may never have to pay it back. Some future model will quietly refactor it all overnight - while we sleep.

Don’t panic. Slop is here, but not for long. Let’s focus on getting through this crazy, ugly period together - and keep our eyes on what comes next.
